The four winners

In 1978 Letraset, Kilkenny Design Workshops and D. O'Sullivan Graphic Supplies asked designers for an Irish typeface. Four designs won and were sold as dry-transfer lettering, two sizes each. As far as we know, three of them had never been digitised before.

an fhianaise

Rebuilt from one flyer

  1. 1 · Source

    One photocopied flyer

    Everything here starts from a single sheet: the flyer that announced the four winners. The scan is 803 × 1125 pixels, about 97 dots per inch, so each printed letter is only 20 to 35 pixels tall.

  2. 2 · Segmentation

    Cut out letter by letter

    Each of the 224 letters, figures and marks on the flyer was cut out and checked by eye. Letters that touched on the sheet were separated along measured cut lines.

  3. 3 · Tracing

    Traced to a fraction of a pixel

    The ink of each letter is upsampled eight times and its edge followed as a sub-pixel contour. Then it is redrawn the way a type designer redraws from a scan: straight stems are straight, curves get as few Bézier points as they need, and corners stay sharp.

  4. 4 · Checking

    Scored against the print

    Every glyph was laid back on the scan, aligned to an eighth of a pixel, and scored by how much of the print and the font overlap. The mean is 0.948, where 1 would be identical. Céilí's h scores lowest, because its printed leg is broken and was rebuilt from the n.

  5. 5 · Compare

    Print on one side, font on the other

    Drag across the picture. To the left of the line is the 1978 print as scanned, toner specks and all. To the right are the outlines the fonts were built from, filled in, sitting exactly where the print was.

  6. 6 · The sheet itself

    A photograph of S53909

    At the foot of the flyer is a photograph of Kilfane's transfer sheet, with the same letters printed dozens of times. Its fine print appears to read ©1973, five years before the competition, which may explain the 1973 dates that some references give.

1 · An chéad duais · First prize

céilí

Designer
Hermann Hecht (1923–1985)
Sheets
S53915 24 pt · S53916 42 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic

A light, calligraphic unicase drawn with a broad flat pen, with insular forms and old-style figures. Hecht was born in Bingen, came to England in 1939, assisted Hans Schleger and later taught at Croydon College of Art. Céilí won him £250 and a silver medallion.

The dancers change into Céilí's alternate letters on the beat: insular r and s, uncial a and n, and its other a, d, e, j, l and v.

2 · An dara duais · Second prize

carrick

Designer
William Curran (1919–2016)
Sheets
S53913 36 pt · S53914 60 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic

A chunky lower-case face, black as a stone. The name comes from carraig, rock. Curran trained as a lithographic artist under Bernardus Romein, worked at Browne & Nolan, and drew the lettering for the school primer An Príméar.

Kilfane

Designer
William Curran
Sheets
S53909 30 pt · S53910 48 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic

Curran's second winner, and the only one of the four with capitals as well as lower case, plus Roman and insular alternates. It is named after Kilfane in County Kilkenny, and is reported on the Series C Irish banknotes designed by Robert Ballagh.

HC · Ardmholadh · Highly commended

tuam uncial

Designer
Jarlath Hayes (1924–2001)
Sheets
S53911 30 pt · S53912 48 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic

A heavy uncial with blunt, square-cut strokes, reportedly made by cutting up Helvetica Bold. Hayes was a book designer and typographer, and he also designed the harp on Ireland's euro coins. Signal Type Foundry has since revived the face commercially as Jarlath.

maisighIlluminate

Insular scribes ringed their initials with dots of red lead and filled the counters with colour. The dots here follow each letter's outline; the knotwork is generated fresh each time.

The designers

William Curran

1919–2016 · Carrick, Kilfane

A lithographic artist trained under Bernardus Romein, then a freelance designer and calligrapher. He drew the lettering for the school primer An Príméar. His archive, with his original drawings for Kilfane and Carrick, went to the National Irish Visual Arts Library in 2025.

hermann hecht

1923–1985 · Céilí

Born in Bingen, he came to England in 1939. He assisted the poster designer Hans Schleger and taught at Croydon College of Art. His papers, with Ann Hecht's, are in the V&A Archive of Art and Design.

jarlath hayes

1924–2001 · Tuam Uncial

Book designer and typographer. Tuam Uncial reportedly appeared on Clannad's record sleeves and on shopfronts, and his harp is on the Irish euro coins. He shared his first name with St Jarlath, the patron saint of Tuam.
