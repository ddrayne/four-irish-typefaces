Na clónna

The four winners

In 1978 Letraset, Kilkenny Design Workshops and D. O'Sullivan Graphic Supplies asked designers for an Irish typeface. The competition brochure lists four awards: a first prize for Céilí (printed there as Caill), Highly Commended for Carrick, and Commended for Kilfane and Tuam Uncial. All four were sold as dry-transfer lettering, in two sizes each.

céilí

Designer
Hermann Hecht (1923–1985)
Sheets
S53915 24 pt · S53916 42 pt
Styles

A light, calligraphic unicase drawn with a broad flat pen, with insular forms and old-style figures. Hecht was born in Bingen, came to England in 1939, assisted Hans Schleger and later taught at Croydon College of Art. Céilí won him £250 and a silver medallion; the brochure prints its name as Caill.

The dancers change into Céilí's alternate letters on the beat: insular r and s, uncial a and n, and its other a, d, e, j, l and v.

A chunky lower-case face, black as a stone. The name comes from carraig, rock. Curran trained as a lithographic artist under Bernardus Romein, worked at Browne & Nolan, and drew the lettering for the school primer An Príméar.

Kilfane

Designer
William Curran
Sheets
S53909 30 pt · S53910 48 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic

Curran's second winner, and the only one of the four with capitals as well as lower case, plus Roman and insular alternates. It is named after Kilfane in County Kilkenny, and is reported on the Series C Irish banknotes designed by Robert Ballagh.

CuimilRub it down

Drag the sheet by its top band to position a letter, then rub over the letter to transfer it. The sheet has no accented letters, only loose accents, as on the flyer: rub one over a vowel to give it a fada. Or type a word and let the machine rub it down.

tuam uncial

Designer
Jarlath Hayes (1924–2001)
Sheets
S53911 30 pt · S53912 48 pt
Styles
Regular, Italic

A heavy uncial with blunt, square-cut strokes, reportedly made by cutting up Helvetica Bold. Hayes was a book designer and typographer, and he also designed the harp on Ireland's euro coins. Signal Type Foundry has since revived the face commercially as Jarlath.

maisighIlluminate

Insular scribes ringed their initials with dots of red lead and filled the counters with colour. The dots here follow each letter's outline; the knotwork is generated fresh each time.

os cionn an dorais

Your name over the door

Irish towns are known for their painted shopfronts: a fascia board between two consoles, the name in gilded letters with a cast shadow, the trade written on the glass. Tuam Uncial went up over real shops. Put your own name up in any of the four.

gach litir

Every character

About four hundred characters in each face: every letter for Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh and the languages of Western and Central Europe, the old dot-above lenition (ḃ ċ ḋ ḟ ġ ṁ ṗ ṡ ṫ), the insular letters and the Tironian ⁊.

Na daoine

The designers

William Curran

1919–2016 · Carrick, Kilfane

A lithographic artist trained under Bernardus Romein, then a freelance designer and calligrapher. He drew the lettering for the school primer An Príméar. His archive, with Kilfane and Carrick specimens and transfer sheets, went to the National Irish Visual Arts Library in 2025.

hermann hecht

1923–1985 · Céilí

Born in Bingen, he came to England in 1939. He assisted the poster designer Hans Schleger and taught at Croydon College of Art. Where his drawings for Céilí are now is not known.

jarlath hayes

1924–2001 · Tuam Uncial

Book designer and typographer. Tuam Uncial reportedly appeared on Clannad's record sleeves and on shopfronts, and his harp is on the Irish euro coins. He shared his first name with St Jarlath, the patron saint of Tuam.

  1. 1973The year in the fine print of the Kilfane sheet photographed on the flyer. A printed copyright line does not date the sheet itself.
  2. 1974An application for Tuam Uncial, years before the competition, is among the material Mary Ann Bolger reproduces in Designing Modern Ireland.
  3. 1978The four winners are announced and sold as transfer sheets S53909–S53916. The competition brochure was designed by Liam Miller and printed at the Dolmen Press.
  4. 1985Hermann Hecht dies, aged 62.
  5. 1992Series C banknotes begin to appear, with lettering reported to be Kilfane.
  6. 2002The euro arrives, with Jarlath Hayes's harp on the Irish coins.
  7. 2020Signal Type Foundry begins Jarlath, a revival of Tuam Uncial, with the Hayes family.
  8. 2025William Curran's archive, with original Kilfane and Carrick material, is deposited at the National Irish Visual Arts Library.

The award wording follows the competition brochure in the William Curran Archive (NIVAL). Other facts come from published sources, and some are reported rather than confirmed. Corrections are welcome on GitHub.
